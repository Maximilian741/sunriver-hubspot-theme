{# ----- CASE 1: 508 audit (featured, open) ----- #}
Accessibility & Compliance · Closed and re-tested

A site nobody could use with a screen reader, made defensible for procurement

The problem

An organization was facing procurement and legal accessibility requirements, with a website that simply could not be used with a screen reader. Missing form labels, focus traps in modals, contrast that almost passed, and documents exported without a single tag. The clock was a contract deadline with real money behind it.

The approach

We ran our three-pass method. First an automated scan to clear the easy wins, knowing tooling catches maybe a third of what is really there. Second a full manual pass: keyboard-only walkthroughs, NVDA and VoiceOver runs, zoom and reflow checks, and PDF tagging with real reading order and honest alt text. Third, the part that matters most, a prioritized remediation report written for the developers who fix it, plus the ACR and VPAT the procurement team actually asked for.

The result

The latest cycle closed and re-tested every critical finding rather than simply logging it. The client walked away with a defensible Accessibility Conformance Report and a site real people can use. See how the audit work runs →

{# ----- CASE 2: OFR to SSRS (pull-quote variant) ----- #}
Data & Reporting · In progress

A portfolio of legacy Oracle reports, rebuilt on SSRS to the penny

The problem

A portfolio of legacy Oracle financial reports was running on aging infrastructure. Finance depended on the numbers daily, but the stack underneath them was getting harder and riskier to keep alive.

Finance does not care that the new stack is cleaner. They care that the new report matches the old one to the penny.

The approach

Inventory first, then rank every report by who actually uses it, because a surprising number have not been opened in years and do not deserve a rebuild. Each surviving report becomes an SSRS RDL, parity-tested side by side against the legacy output before anyone cuts over. Subscriptions and schedules move last, once the numbers have earned trust.

The result

In progress, and honest about it: the full inventory is done and the first tranche of reports is through parity testing and ready to cut over. See the database and reporting work →

{# ----- CASE 3: Vital Tails (list variant, our own product) ----- #}
Product · Our own build, in progress

Vital Tails: pet health records out of the shoebox

The problem

Pet health records live in a shoebox of paper. The moment you need a rabies certificate is rarely the moment you can find it, and almost never the moment you have good signal.

The approach

A mobile-first, offline-friendly companion app, built the way we build for clients: accessible from the first commit. It keeps the things every owner loses track of in one place you can share with your vet.

  • Health records and vaccine history, on the phone in your pocket
  • Vaccine and medication reminders before the dates sneak up
  • Weight tracked over time, not guessed at
  • Clean vet-sharing instead of a folder of photos of paper

The result

In progress: core records and reminders work today, and vet-sharing is next on the bench. Our quality assurance department, Noki and Moggy, are the first two registered patients. Beta news lands soon. See the app development work →

{# ----- CASE 4: Hood-cleaning app ----- #}
Field Service · Working prototype

A field-service app shaped around the truck and the overnight job

The problem

Kitchen-exhaust cleaning is regulated, photographed, and scheduled for 2 a.m., and most crews still run it on paper and group texts. The documentation inspectors and insurers expect ends up reconstructed days later from memory.

The approach

An app built around how the job actually runs: recurring scheduling that understands service intervals, on-site before and after photo capture tied to each system, service reports generated on the spot in the spirit of NFPA 96 documentation, and a clean handoff to invoicing so the paperwork is done when the truck door closes.

The result

In progress: a working prototype, being shaped against real routes and real overnight jobs. If your operation runs on a group chat, this is the shape of the fix. See the app development work →
