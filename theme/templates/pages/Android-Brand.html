What we focus on

We write in idiomatic Kotlin and build the whole UI in Jetpack Compose, so there are no XML layouts to keep in sync with code. State is driven by coroutines and Kotlin Flow exposed from ViewModels, which keeps the main thread free and makes the data flow easy to test. The result is fewer lines than the old View system and far less boilerplate to maintain over the app’s life.
Material 3 is more than a coat of paint, we wire up dynamic color (Material You) so the palette adapts to the user’s wallpaper on Android 12 and up, with a sensible fallback theme below that. Type and spacing follow the Material 3 scale rather than hand-picked pixel values, and we build the dark theme and TalkBack content descriptions in from the first screen instead of retrofitting them. That means proper contrast, focus order, and large-text support without a separate accessibility pass at the end.
We enroll the app in Play App Signing so Google holds the upload key securely and you are never one lost keystore away from being unable to update. Releases go out as Android App Bundles (.aab) through staged rollouts, starting at a small percentage and watching crash and ANR rates in the Play Console before going wider. You keep full ownership of the Play account and release tracks; we operate the pipeline, but nothing about it is a black box you can’t see into.
Deferrable jobs, uploads, syncs, periodic refreshes, run through WorkManager, which respects Doze and battery limits and survives reboots and process death. Local data lives in a Room database (SQLite under the hood) so the app is fully usable with no signal, and we design an explicit sync strategy with conflict resolution rather than blindly overwriting. When connectivity returns, queued changes reconcile cleanly instead of silently dropping a user’s work.
We generate Baseline Profiles so the most-used code paths are ahead-of-time compiled on first launch, which measurably cuts cold-start and jank on cheaper hardware. Cold-start and frame timings are tracked against an explicit budget using Macrobenchmark, run on real low-end devices rather than a high-end emulator. That keeps the app feeling responsive on a three-year-old phone, which is what most of your users are actually holding.

Why it matters

Declarative UI in Compose with coroutines and Flow for state, so screens stay responsive and the codebase stays small.

Dynamic color, typography scales, and motion that follow Material 3, plus dark theme and TalkBack support from day one.

Play App Signing, staged rollouts, and a signed App Bundle (.aab) through a release track you control, not a black box.

WorkManager for deferrable jobs and a Room-backed local store that syncs cleanly when the network comes back.

Baseline Profiles, cold-start budgets, and Macrobenchmark runs so the app feels fast on 3-year-old hardware, not just new.
