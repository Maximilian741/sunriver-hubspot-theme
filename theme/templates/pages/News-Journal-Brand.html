The method · Why a logbook

My family has worked this valley for five generations, and a working mine runs on one document above all others: the logbook. You assay the rock before you spend a season chasing it. You write down what the ground actually gave up, and you keep that record honest. And when you move on, you leave the workings safer than you found them.

Software is the same craft on different ground. So this page is run like a claim ledger. Every entry below is a real seam of work, dated, with an honest read on where it stands today. These are simply the notes I keep at the bench, made public, and they skip the press-release gloss entirely.

June 2026 · Compliance

508 audit cycle closed, every critical finding re-tested

Wrapped the spring accessibility cycle. The method runs in three passes: an automated scan to map the easy wins (tooling catches maybe a third of what is really there), then a manual pass with NVDA, VoiceOver, full keyboard runs, zoom and reflow checks, and PDF tagging so documents read in the right order; then a remediation report written for the developers who have to fix it, with the ACR and VPAT procurement teams ask for. The milestone worth logging: every critical finding was closed and re-tested, right down to a clean re-scan. If you answer to Section 508 or want WCAG 2.2 AA because it is simply right, this is the exact work I do.

May 2026 · Data & reporting

OFR to SSRS: first tranche through parity, to the penny

Mid-flight on moving a portfolio of legacy Oracle financial reports onto SQL Server Reporting Services. These migrations live or die on one word, parity. Finance cares about one thing above the cleaner stack: that the new number matches the old number exactly. The work so far: a full inventory ranked by who actually opens each report (a fair number had sat untouched for years and quietly earned retirement instead of migration), every data source mapped, queries rebuilt, and layouts recreated as RDLs. Status logged: inventory complete, first tranche through side-by-side parity testing with the legacy output. Subscriptions and schedules move last, once the numbers have earned trust. If you are staring down a legacy reporting stack, this is a lane I know well.

May 2026 · Product

Vital Tails: core records and reminders working, vet-sharing on the bench

Vital Tails is our own product, a pet-care companion for the things every owner loses track of: health records, vaccine and medication reminders, weight over time, all in one place you can hand to your vet instead of digging through a shoebox of paperwork. It is built the way I build for clients, mobile-first, accessible from day one, and offline-friendly, because the moment you need a rabies certificate is rarely the moment you have good signal. Logged this month: core records and reminders are working today, and vet-sharing is next up. Quality assurance, Noki and Moggy, are the first two registered patients, and remain unimpressed by deadlines. Beta news will land on this seam first.

April 2026 · Field service

Hood-cleaning crew app: working prototype, tested on real 2 a.m. routes

Kitchen exhaust cleaning is regulated, photographic, and scheduled in the dead of night, and most crews still run it on paper and group texts. This app is shaped around how the job actually goes: scheduling that understands recurring service intervals, before and after photo capture tied to each system, service reports generated on the spot in the spirit of NFPA 96 documentation, and a clean handoff to invoicing so the paperwork is finished when the truck door closes rather than three days later. Status logged: a working prototype, being shaped against real routes and real 2 a.m. jobs. If you run a field crew and your “system” is a chat thread, this is the kind of app I build.
