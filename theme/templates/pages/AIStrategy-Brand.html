What we focus on

We run a short, structured discovery: interview the people who do the work, map the steps end to end, and score each candidate use case on business value, technical feasibility, and risk. The output is a ranked shortlist with the weak ideas explicitly cut and a one-page rationale for each survivor, so the conversation moves from “everyone’s doing AI” to “these three tasks, in this order, for these reasons.”
We build a real cost model instead of a hopeful one: token and API spend at expected volume, build and maintenance effort, and the cost of the human review that production AI still requires. We weigh that against measured time saved and error reduction, and we’re comfortable recommending against a project — a thin margin, a task too high-stakes to automate, or a problem a cheaper rule would solve — because a consultant who never says don’t isn’t giving you advice.
The roadmap is staged on purpose: a contained pilot with a defined success metric and a fixed evaluation, a decision gate where we keep, fix, or stop, and only then a scaled rollout of what the pilot actually proved. That sequencing caps your downside — a pilot that disappoints costs a small scope, not a year and a platform — and it gives leadership a concrete result to judge before any larger commitment.
We give you a defended build-versus-buy-versus-wait call for each use case. Off-the-shelf wins when a mature tool already fits and the problem isn’t a differentiator; building on the Claude or OpenAI API wins when you need control, custom data, or your own guardrails; waiting wins when the capability is improving fast or prices are dropping monthly. We write down the tradeoffs in cost, control, switching risk, and vendor lock-in so the decision is yours and it’s documented.
Adoption is where most AI efforts quietly die, so we treat it as part of the work, not an afterthought. That means involving the people who’ll use the tool early, training tailored to their actual tasks, a feedback channel so friction gets fixed instead of routed around, and metrics that track real usage rather than license counts. A capable tool nobody trusts returns nothing, and we’d rather ship something smaller that people genuinely adopt.

Why it matters

We map your workflows and score candidate use cases on value, feasibility, and risk — so effort goes to the handful that pay off, not the ones that just sound impressive.

We model real costs — tokens, build, and the human review AI still needs — against the time it saves, and we tell you plainly when the smart move is to skip it.

A sequenced plan that starts with a low-risk pilot, defines what success looks like, and only scales what the pilot proved — not a big-bang rollout on a hope.

A clear recommendation on whether to use an off-the-shelf tool, build on an API, or hold off — with the tradeoffs in cost, control, and lock-in written down.

Rollout, training, and feedback loops built in, because a tool nobody trusts or uses returns nothing — adoption is where most AI projects quietly fail.
