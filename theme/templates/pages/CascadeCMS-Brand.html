What we focus on

We build region-based templates and configuration sets so a single layout governs markup across thousands of pages, and write XSLT formats that transform structured data definitions into clean, consistent HTML. Velocity is used where it earns its place, but the heavy structural logic lives in XSLT that is version-controlled and reviewable. Editors never touch raw markup, so a brand or accessibility fix made once propagates everywhere.
We model content with Cascade data definitions and asset factories so authors fill in named fields — headings, dates, links, media — instead of pasting fragile HTML. Asset factory containers enforce where new content can be created and which template it inherits, keeping the structure intact as the site grows. This is what makes large sites maintainable: the content type, not the author, owns the markup.
We have worked inside the governance reality of universities and public agencies, where dozens of departments publish into one site under shared standards. Site sections, groups, and role-based permissions are mapped to your actual org chart, and approval workflows route content to the right reviewer before publish. The setup respects decentralized authorship while keeping central control over templates, accessibility, and brand.
We inventory legacy content, map each page type into its target Cascade data definition, and script the migration so structure carries over rather than copy-pasting into a void. Redirects are generated from the old URL scheme to the new one, and we verify internal link integrity so nothing 404s after cutover. Search rankings and bookmarks survive the move because the URL and redirect strategy is planned up front, not patched afterward.
We build WCAG 2.1 AA into the templates themselves — semantic landmarks, correct heading order, accessible names on controls, sufficient contrast, and keyboard-navigable components — so compliance is the default rather than a per-page chore. Editor field guidance prompts authors for alt text and descriptive link text at the point of entry. Because the accessible markup lives in the template, a routine content update cannot quietly break conformance, which is what keeps Section 508 and ADA obligations satisfied over time.

Why it matters

Region-based templates and XSLT formats that keep markup consistent across thousands of managed pages.
