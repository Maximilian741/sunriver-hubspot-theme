{# --- LEAD: 508 --- #} June 2026 · Compliance · The lead

Section 508, in practice: what an audit actually looks like up close

Most teams meet Section 508 the hard way: a procurement checklist, a hard deadline, and a PDF that a screen reader stumbles through. I have spent this spring deep in 508 and WCAG 2.2 work, and the pattern is the same on every contract. The failures are rarely exotic. They are missing form labels, focus traps in modals, color contrast that almost passes, and documents exported without a single tag. My cycle runs in three passes: an automated scan to map the easy wins, since tooling catches maybe a third of what is really there; then a manual pass with NVDA and VoiceOver, full keyboard runs, zoom and reflow checks, and PDF tagging so documents read in the right order.

The third pass is the one that matters: a prioritized remediation report written for the developers who have to fix it, with the ACR and VPAT documentation procurement teams ask for. The milestone worth logging this cycle is plain. Every critical finding was closed and re-tested, down to a clean re-scan, then signed off rather than merely filed. If your organization answers to 508, or simply wants WCAG 2.2 AA because it is the right thing to do, this is exactly the work I do.

{# --- OFR -> SSRS --- #} May 2026 · Data & Reporting

OFR to SSRS: migrating legacy financial reports while keeping every number

I am mid-flight on a reporting migration, moving a portfolio of legacy Oracle financial reports onto SQL Server Reporting Services. These projects live or die on one word: parity. To finance, the only thing that counts is that the new report matches the old one to the penny, every line, every rounding rule, every subtotal. So the work starts slow and honest. I inventory every report and rank it by who actually opens it, and a fair number had sat untouched for years and quietly earned retirement instead of migration.

From there it is steady seam work: map each data source, rebuild the queries, recreate layouts as RDLs, then run side-by-side parity testing against the legacy output before anything goes live. Subscriptions and schedules move last, once the numbers have earned trust. Status this issue: the inventory is complete and the first tranche of reports is through parity, matched to the penny. If you are staring down a legacy reporting stack, Oracle or otherwise, this is a lane I know well.

{# --- VITAL TAILS --- #} May 2026 · Product

Vital Tails: a pet-care companion I am building, and using on my own two cats

Vital Tails is my own product build, a pet-care companion for the things every owner loses track of: health records, vaccine and medication reminders, weight over time, all in one place you can hand to your vet instead of digging through a shoebox of paperwork. It is built the way I build for clients, mobile-first, accessible from day one, and offline-friendly, because the moment you need a rabies certificate is usually the moment signal drops in a parking lot.

Logged this issue: core records and reminders work today, and vet-sharing is next on the bench. The quality assurance department, Noki and Moggy, are the first two registered patients and remain deeply unimpressed by deadlines. Beta news will land on this kind of work first, so this is the page to watch.

{# --- HOOD CLEANING --- #} April 2026 · Field Service

A job app for hood-cleaning crews: built around the truck, not the office

Kitchen exhaust cleaning is regulated, photographic, and scheduled in the dead of night, and most crews still run it on paper and group texts. I am building a field-service app shaped around how the job actually goes. It has scheduling that understands recurring service intervals, on-site before and after photo capture tied to each system, and service reports generated on the spot in the spirit of NFPA 96 documentation, the kind inspectors and insurers actually ask for.

The last piece is a clean handoff to invoicing, so the paperwork is finished when the truck door closes rather than three days later from somebody's kitchen table. Status this issue: a working prototype, being shaped against real routes and real 2 a.m. jobs. If you run a field crew of any kind and your current system is a group chat, this is the kind of app I build.
