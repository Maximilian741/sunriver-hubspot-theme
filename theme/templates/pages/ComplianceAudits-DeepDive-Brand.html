Compliance Audits / Penetration Testing

We attack it before an attacker does.

Manual, senior-led penetration testing across your applications, network, and APIs — mapped to OWASP, evidenced with proof-of-concept, and written up so your engineers can fix it fast.

ScopeWeb & App PentestNetwork & ExternalAPI TestingSocial EngineeringRemediation Report

What we focus on

Testing follows the OWASP Web Security Testing Guide (WSTG) and is mapped to the OWASP Top 10 and ASVS verification levels, covering injection (SQLi, NoSQLi, command), broken access control, authentication and session flaws, and business-logic abuse that scanners miss. We exercise both authenticated roles and unauthenticated paths, fuzzing inputs with Burp Suite Pro and validating each finding by hand to eliminate false positives. Every issue ships with a proof-of-concept request, an impact rating, and the specific control or code change that closes it.
External testing enumerates your internet-facing perimeter — open ports, exposed admin interfaces, TLS and certificate hygiene, and default or weak credentials — while internal testing simulates a foothold to chart lateral movement, privilege escalation, and segmentation gaps. We use Nmap, OpenVAS or Nessus for discovery and then manual exploitation to confirm what is genuinely reachable, not just theoretically vulnerable. Deliverables include a host-by-host findings matrix and a prioritized hardening plan aligned to CIS Benchmarks.
REST and GraphQL endpoints are assessed against the OWASP API Security Top 10, with emphasis on Broken Object-Level Authorization (BOLA), Broken Object Property-Level Authorization (mass assignment), and missing function-level checks. We import OpenAPI or Swagger definitions, replay and tamper requests in Postman and Burp, and probe rate limiting, token scoping, and introspection exposure on GraphQL. The report documents each broken authorization path with the exact request, the data it exposed, and the server-side fix.
Phishing and pretext campaigns are scoped and authorized in a signed rules-of-engagement document, then executed using frameworks such as GoPhish with cloned landing pages and tracked payloads to measure click, credential-submission, and reporting rates. Engagements may include vishing or physical pretexting where authorized, all conducted under explicit legal sign-off and a defined safe-word and escalation path. You receive metrics benchmarked against industry baselines plus targeted security-awareness recommendations.
Findings are ranked by real-world risk using CVSS v3.1 base and environmental scores combined with exploitability and business context, not raw scanner severity. Each entry includes step-by-step reproduction, evidence, affected assets, and a concrete remediation with code or configuration guidance, summarized in an executive narrative for leadership and a technical appendix for engineers. After you remediate, we re-test all critical and high findings at no extra charge and issue an attestation of the fixes verified.

Compliance Audits / Threat Modeling

Design out the risk before you build.

Structured threat modeling that maps how your system can be abused — STRIDE, data-flow diagrams, and a prioritized set of mitigations your team can act on at design time, when fixes are cheap.

ScopeSTRIDE AnalysisData-Flow DiagramsAttack SurfacePrioritized MitigationsDesign-Phase Reviews

What we focus on

We decompose the system and walk every trust boundary through the six STRIDE categories — Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, and Elevation of privilege — to surface threats systematically rather than relying on intuition. Sessions are run collaboratively with your architects, often using the Microsoft Threat Modeling Tool or OWASP Threat Dragon to capture threats against each element and flow. The output is a structured threat register linking every threat to the asset, entry point, and boundary it affects.
We build Data-Flow Diagrams to a consistent notation — external entities, processes, data stores, and flows, with trust boundaries drawn explicitly — so it is unambiguous where data originates, where it rests, and which boundaries it traverses. These DFDs become the shared reference for the whole engagement and live alongside your architecture docs so they can be versioned and updated as the design evolves. Each crossing of a trust boundary is flagged as a candidate location for authentication, validation, or encryption controls.
We compile an attack-surface inventory enumerating every entry point, API, third-party dependency, and privileged execution path, drawing on the OWASP Attack Surface Analysis Cheat Sheet. Dependencies are reviewed for known-vulnerability and supply-chain exposure, and privileged or trust-elevating paths are highlighted so high-value targets are explicit. The deliverable is a catalogued surface map that makes it clear where an attacker would actually push and what would be reachable if a component fell.
Each identified threat is rated for likelihood and impact — using DREAD or a CVSS-aligned qualitative scale — and paired with a specific mitigating control mapped where useful to OWASP ASVS or NIST SP 800-53. The result is a ranked backlog that tells engineering which design changes buy down the most risk per unit of effort, so scarce time goes to what matters first. Residual risk for each threat is stated explicitly so leadership can make an informed accept-or-fix decision.
We embed in early architecture and feature design reviews to catch flawed trust assumptions, missing authorization, and unsafe defaults while they are still cheap whiteboard changes rather than post-release incidents. This shift-left practice plugs threat modeling into your SDLC and design gates, producing lightweight, repeatable model updates as features evolve. Each review yields documented design recommendations and security acceptance criteria your team can carry straight into implementation.

Compliance Audits / WCAG 2.2

Accessible to everyone, proven not assumed.

WCAG 2.0 through 2.2 conformance audits driven by real assistive-technology testing — not just an automated scan. You get a defensible VPAT and a remediation list your developers can work straight through.

ScopeWCAG 2.2 AA ConformanceScreen-Reader TestingKeyboard NavigationColor ContrastVPAT & Statement

What we focus on

We evaluate every applicable success criterion against WCAG 2.2 Level AA, including the nine new 2.2 criteria such as Focus Not Obscured, Target Size (Minimum), Dragging Movements, and Accessible Authentication. Testing combines automated tooling (axe-core, WAVE, Lighthouse) for coverage with extensive manual inspection, since roughly half of WCAG criteria cannot be verified by automation alone. Each non-conformance is logged with the failing criterion, conformance level, severity, and a recommended fix referencing the relevant W3C technique.
Content is exercised hands-on with multiple screen readers — NVDA and JAWS on Windows, VoiceOver on macOS and iOS — to confirm that name, role, and value are announced correctly and that reading order, headings, landmarks, and live-region updates are coherent aloud. We verify that dynamic components and ARIA usage follow the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices rather than just passing a parser. Findings note the specific assistive technology and browser pairing so developers can reproduce each issue.
We perform complete keyboard-only walkthroughs to confirm that all functionality is operable without a mouse, that focus order is logical and matches visual order, that focus is always visible, and that no keyboard traps exist. Custom widgets — menus, modals, carousels, comboboxes — are checked against expected ARIA keyboard interaction patterns for arrow, Escape, and Enter handling. Each defect maps to criteria such as 2.1.1 Keyboard, 2.4.3 Focus Order, and 2.4.7 Focus Visible with a concrete remediation.
Text, meaningful graphics, and UI components are measured against WCAG contrast minimums — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for non-text contrast under 1.4.11 — using tools like the TPGi Colour Contrast Analyser and computed-value sampling. We test against actual rendered states including hover, focus, disabled, and overlay-on-image cases that static palettes overlook. Deliverables include a contrast findings table and adjusted color values that preserve your brand while meeting the threshold.
We produce a completed VPAT 2.5 (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) using the WCAG edition, with honest Supports / Partially Supports / Does Not Support conformance statements and explanatory remarks for each criterion. Alongside it we draft a public-facing accessibility statement modeled on the W3C template, documenting conformance scope, known limitations, and a feedback channel. Both artifacts are written to withstand procurement diligence and legal scrutiny, including ADA Title III and Section 508 contexts.

Compliance Audits / 508 Compliance

Section 508 conformance, documented for procurement.

Federal Section 508 and ICT accessibility audits — web, software, and documents — with an Accessibility Conformance Report your contracting officer can rely on, plus the remediation to back it up.

ScopeFederal 508 / ICTDocument & PDF AccessibilityACR / VPATProcurement SupportRemediation

What we focus on

We audit against the Revised Section 508 Standards (36 CFR Part 1194), which incorporate WCAG 2.0 Level AA by reference as the baseline for web and electronic content, plus the functional performance criteria and hardware provisions of the ICT standards. Coverage spans web interfaces, software and authoring tools, and any hardware or self-contained device controls in scope. Results are organized by the standard’s chapters so an agency reviewer can trace conformance clause by clause.
Documents are remediated to PDF/UA and the Section 508 document requirements — correct tag structure and reading order, alternative text, heading hierarchy, table header associations, list semantics, language metadata, and accessible form fields. We validate with the PAC (PDF Accessibility Checker), Adobe Acrobat Pro’s checker, and manual screen-reader review, since automated checks alone do not confirm logical reading order. You receive remediated source and output files plus a checklist evidencing each requirement met.
We deliver an Accessibility Conformance Report built on the current ITI VPAT 2.5 template, Section 508 edition, with per-criterion ratings of Supports, Partially Supports, Does Not Support, or Not Applicable and substantive remarks justifying each call. The ACR covers the WCAG 2.0 AA tables, the functional performance criteria, hardware, software, and support-documentation chapters as applicable. It is written to give a contracting officer defensible, evidence-backed conformance language for the procurement file.
We help vendors and agencies interpret, draft, and defend 508 language across the acquisition lifecycle — market research, Section 508 solicitation clauses (FAR 39.2), evaluation of submitted ACRs, and acceptance criteria for deliverables. For agencies we assess whether supplier conformance claims are credible; for vendors we prepare responses that map products honestly to the standards. The aim is to reduce procurement risk and avoid accessibility findings surfacing after award.
Remediation is delivered as a prioritized plan with code-level guidance — specific HTML, ARIA, and document-tagging changes tied to each failed provision — sequenced so the highest-impact barriers are resolved first. We support developers through implementation and then re-test the fixes so the conformance claim in the ACR is backed by verified evidence rather than intent. A final attestation records what was retested and confirmed, keeping the claim defensible under audit.
