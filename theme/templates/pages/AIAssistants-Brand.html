What we focus on

Retrieval-augmented generation is what separates an assistant that knows your business from one that confidently guesses. We chunk your documents thoughtfully, generate embeddings, and store them in a vector database (pgvector, Chroma, Pinecone, or similar), then at query time we retrieve the most relevant passages and feed them to the model as context. We tune chunk size, overlap, and retrieval count for your content, and add reranking or hybrid keyword-plus-vector search where pure similarity falls short — because retrieval quality is what makes or breaks the whole system.
Citations aren’t decoration — they’re how an assistant earns trust. We carry source metadata through retrieval so every answer can point back to the exact document and passage it drew from, surfaced as a link or snippet the user can open. That also makes hallucinations visible: if an answer can’t cite a source, that’s a signal to distrust it, and it gives your team a fast way to verify rather than taking the model’s word.
Guardrails keep the assistant useful and safe. We write a tight system prompt defining its role and limits, add input handling to resist prompt injection, and constrain output so it stays on the topics your data covers. Crucially, we tune it to say “I don’t know” or “that’s not in our documents” when retrieval comes up empty, because an assistant that admits the gap is far more valuable — and safer — than one that fills it with a plausible fabrication.
We build on the Claude and OpenAI APIs and choose deliberately rather than defaulting. A larger model like Claude Opus or GPT-class flagship handles nuanced reasoning; a smaller, faster model handles routine lookups at a fraction of the cost and latency. We often route by difficulty, cache stable context to cut spend, and stream responses for a responsive feel. The model is a swappable component, so as better or cheaper options ship, we move without rebuilding the whole assistant.
We don’t ship on three lucky demos. We assemble an evaluation set of real questions paired with known-good answers and reference sources, then score the assistant for answer accuracy, faithfulness to the retrieved context, and whether citations actually support the claim. That gives a baseline number, catches regressions when we change a prompt or model, and turns “it feels right” into evidence you can stand behind — and that we re-run whenever something in the pipeline changes.

Why it matters

We index your documents into a vector store and retrieve the right passages at answer time, so responses come from your content instead of the model’s imagination.

Every answer links back to the source passage it came from, so users can check the original and trust the assistant instead of guessing whether it’s right.

System prompts, input and output checks, and refusal behavior that keep the assistant on-topic and honest — including saying “I don’t know” when the answer isn’t in your data.

We build on the Claude or OpenAI API and pick the model per job, balancing quality, latency, and cost rather than defaulting to the most expensive option for everything.

A test set of real questions and expected answers, scored for accuracy and grounding, so you ship on measured quality — not a vibe from three lucky demos.
