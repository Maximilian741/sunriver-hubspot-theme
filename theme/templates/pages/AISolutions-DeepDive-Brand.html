Most AI budgets get spent chasing demos that never reach production. We start from your actual work, pick the few use cases worth doing, and give you a roadmap with honest ROI and a plan for the people who have to use it.

What we focus on

We run a short, structured discovery: interview the people who do the work, map the steps end to end, and score each candidate use case on business value, technical feasibility, and risk. The output is a ranked shortlist with the weak ideas explicitly cut and a one-page rationale for each survivor, so the conversation moves from “everyone’s doing AI” to “these three tasks, in this order, for these reasons.”
We build a real cost model instead of a hopeful one: token and API spend at expected volume, build and maintenance effort, and the cost of the human review that production AI still requires. We weigh that against measured time saved and error reduction, and we’re comfortable recommending against a project, a thin margin, a task too high-stakes to automate, or a problem a cheaper rule would solve, because a consultant who never says don’t isn’t giving you advice.
The roadmap is staged on purpose: a contained pilot with a defined success metric and a fixed evaluation, a decision gate where we keep, fix, or stop, and only then a scaled rollout of what the pilot actually proved. That sequencing caps your downside, a pilot that disappoints costs a small scope, not a year and a platform, and it gives leadership a concrete result to judge before any larger commitment.
We give you a defended build-versus-buy-versus-wait call for each use case. Off-the-shelf wins when a mature tool already fits and the problem isn’t a differentiator; building on the Claude or OpenAI API wins when you need control, custom data, or your own guardrails; waiting wins when the capability is improving fast or prices are dropping monthly. We write down the tradeoffs in cost, control, switching risk, and vendor lock-in so the decision is yours and it’s documented.
Adoption is where most AI efforts quietly die, so we treat it as part of the work, not an afterthought. That means involving the people who’ll use the tool early, training tailored to their actual tasks, a feedback channel so friction gets fixed instead of routed around, and metrics that track real usage rather than license counts. A capable tool nobody trusts returns nothing, and we’d rather ship something smaller that people genuinely adopt.

Generic chatbots make things up because they’ve never seen your documents. We build assistants grounded in your own content through retrieval, with citations you can check, guardrails that keep them on task, and evaluations that prove they work before you ship.

ScopeGrounded in your data (RAG)Citations you can verifyGuardrails & scopeClaude & OpenAI APIsEvaluation before launch

What we focus on

Retrieval-augmented generation is what separates an assistant that knows your business from one that confidently guesses. We chunk your documents thoughtfully, generate embeddings, and store them in a vector database (pgvector, Chroma, Pinecone, or similar), then at query time we retrieve the most relevant passages and feed them to the model as context. We tune chunk size, overlap, and retrieval count for your content, and add reranking or hybrid keyword-plus-vector search where pure similarity falls short, because retrieval quality is what makes or breaks the whole system.
Citations aren’t decoration, they’re how an assistant earns trust. We carry source metadata through retrieval so every answer can point back to the exact document and passage it drew from, surfaced as a link or snippet the user can open. That also makes hallucinations visible: if an answer can’t cite a source, that’s a signal to distrust it, and it gives your team a fast way to verify rather than taking the model’s word.
Guardrails keep the assistant useful and safe. We write a tight system prompt defining its role and limits, add input handling to resist prompt injection, and constrain output so it stays on the topics your data covers. Crucially, we tune it to say “I don’t know” or “that’s not in our documents” when retrieval comes up empty, because an assistant that admits the gap is far more valuable, and safer, than one that fills it with a plausible fabrication.
We build on the Claude and OpenAI APIs and choose deliberately rather than defaulting. A larger model like Claude Opus or GPT-class flagship handles nuanced reasoning; a smaller, faster model handles routine lookups at a fraction of the cost and latency. We often route by difficulty, cache stable context to cut spend, and stream responses for a responsive feel. The model is a swappable component, so as better or cheaper options ship, we move without rebuilding the whole assistant.
We don’t ship on three lucky demos. We assemble an evaluation set of real questions paired with known-good answers and reference sources, then score the assistant for answer accuracy, faithfulness to the retrieved context, and whether citations actually support the claim. That gives a baseline number, catches regressions when we change a prompt or model, and turns “it feels right” into evidence you can stand behind, and that we re-run whenever something in the pipeline changes.

The biggest AI wins are usually boring: the repetitive triage, sorting, and drafting that eats your team’s day. We automate that work with tool-using agents and dependable integrations, keep a person on the decisions that matter, and build for the days a step fails.

ScopeAutomate repetitive workTool-using agentsHuman in the loopIntegrations: n8n, Zapier & customReliability & observability

What we focus on

We hunt for the high-volume, low-judgment work that drains hours without needing a human brain: triaging inbound email and tickets, extracting fields from invoices or forms, classifying and tagging records, drafting routine replies, and summarizing long threads. These tasks are ideal for AI because the patterns are consistent and the stakes per item are low, and clawing back those hours is usually a bigger, faster win than any flashy customer-facing feature.
Agents earn their keep when they take action, not just talk. Using frameworks like LangChain (or lighter custom orchestration), we give a model a defined set of tools, search your knowledge base, call an internal API, read or update a record, send a draft for approval, and a narrow, well-specified job. We deliberately keep scope tight: a focused agent that does one workflow reliably beats an open-ended “do anything” agent that’s impressive in a demo and unpredictable in production.
We design human-in-the-loop checkpoints wherever a mistake would be costly or hard to undo. The automation does the heavy lifting, gathering, drafting, proposing, and a person approves before anything irreversible happens, like sending an external message, moving money, or changing a customer record. We set confidence thresholds so routine, high-certainty cases can flow through while edge cases and low-confidence outputs get escalated, keeping a named human accountable for the decisions that matter.
We meet you where your stack already is. For many workflows, n8n (which we can self-host for data control) or Zapier connects the pieces quickly and stays maintainable by your team. When reliability, complex branching, or sensitive data demands it, we write custom integration code against the APIs directly. We choose per workflow rather than forcing one approach, and we’re honest about when a no-code tool is the pragmatic answer and when it would become a liability.
An automation you can’t observe is a liability, so we build in operational basics from day one: retries with backoff for transient failures, sensible fallbacks when a model or API is down, structured logging of inputs, outputs, and decisions, and alerts when error rates climb or a step stalls. AI calls fail and models change, so we add idempotency to prevent double-processing and a clear audit trail, the difference between a system you trust unattended and one that silently corrupts data.

The gap isn’t access to AI, nearly everyone has it now, it’s knowing how to use it well and when not to trust it. We coach individuals and teams in plain language, with hands-on practice on your real work, no technical background assumed.

ScopePrompting that worksEveryday workflowsJudgment: when to trust itApproachable for everyoneReference materials that last

What we focus on

We teach prompting as a practical skill, not a list of magic words. People learn to give clear context and constraints, show the model an example of what good looks like, ask for structured output, and iterate when the first answer misses, using the model’s response to refine the next prompt. We practice on the attendees’ own tasks, whether that’s drafting a report, cleaning a spreadsheet, or summarizing a document, so the skill transfers directly to Monday morning instead of staying abstract.
We help each role identify the handful of AI uses that genuinely save them time, because a tool tried once and forgotten changes nothing. That might be a support rep drafting first-pass replies, an analyst explaining a formula, or a manager turning rough notes into a clean summary. We then help build those uses into repeatable habits, saved prompts, a known sequence of steps, so AI becomes part of the actual workflow rather than a novelty people drift away from.
The single most valuable thing we teach is judgment: AI states wrong answers with the same confidence as right ones, so people need to know when to verify and when not to rely on it at all. We cover how to spot likely fabrication, which kinds of work demand a human check (anything legal, financial, medical, or fact-critical), and how to use AI as a capable draft-maker and thinking partner while keeping a human firmly responsible for the result. That skepticism is what separates productive use from costly mistakes.
We pitch sessions for non-technical people deliberately, no jargon, no assumptions, and plenty of hands-on practice, because the people who stand to gain most are often the least likely to experiment on their own. We work at a comfortable pace, answer the questions people are sometimes embarrassed to ask, and focus on building confidence so the whole team levels up together rather than leaving AI to a few self-taught enthusiasts while everyone else falls behind.
Skills fade without reinforcement, so attendees leave with materials built for them: cheat sheets of effective prompt patterns, a library of templates for their common tasks, and a short internal guide tailored to the specific tools your organization uses. We can also help set light internal norms, what’s fine to use AI for, what needs review, what data to keep out, so the training compounds into a shared practice instead of evaporating a week after the session.

Plenty of useful AI never has to send your data to anyone. We run models locally with Ollama, configure API setups that never train on your inputs, and handle PII deliberately, so sensitive work stays inside a boundary you control and can prove.

ScopeLocal & self-hosted modelsNo training on your dataPII handlingCompliance-aware designRight-sized to the job

What we focus on

Running open models locally with Ollama means your prompts and documents never leave hardware you control, no third party ever sees the data. We deploy capable open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, and similar) sized to your machines, from a workstation to a GPU server, and wire them into the same kinds of assistants and automations we build on hosted APIs. For confidential, regulated, or air-gapped work, this is often the only acceptable architecture, and modern open models are genuinely good enough for most everyday tasks.
When a hosted API is the right tool for the quality you need, the default consumer terms aren’t the only option. We configure enterprise and API-tier access from Anthropic and OpenAI for zero data retention and no training on your inputs, and we document the exact setting and the provider’s data-handling commitment so the guarantee is verifiable rather than assumed. We make the distinction explicit: the free consumer chatbot and a properly configured API account have very different data postures, and we set up the latter.
We treat personal data as something to minimize before it ever reaches a model. Depending on the workflow, that means redacting or masking PII at the boundary, names, account numbers, health or financial details, sending only what the task genuinely requires, and keeping identifiers on your side where they belong. We document what is transmitted and what is withheld so you have a clear, defensible record of how personal data flows through the system, which is exactly what a privacy review or breach inquiry will ask for.
We design with your compliance obligations in view, HIPAA, FERPA, attorney-client confidentiality, and similar regimes, rather than bolting controls on afterward. That covers where data physically lives, who and what can access the model and logs, encryption in transit and at rest, and an audit trail of usage. We’re not your lawyers and we’ll say so, but we build the architecture to support your requirements and document the design decisions so your compliance team and auditors can review the reasoning, not just the result.
Privacy and cost both improve when the model is right-sized to the job. A small local model handles the routine, high-volume cases entirely on your infrastructure, private by construction and free of per-call fees, while you reserve a hosted frontier model for the genuinely hard tasks that need its reasoning. We’ll often design a hybrid: sensitive or bulk work stays local, and only carefully scrubbed, non-sensitive requests go to an external API, giving you strong privacy without giving up capability where it counts.

Shipping an AI feature is the start, not the finish. We measure how accurate it really is, check for hallucination, bias, and unsafe output, and leave you a usage policy and monitoring, so you can answer for what your AI does long after launch day.

ScopeAccuracy & hallucination testingBias & safety checksUsage policyMonitoring & driftResponsible-use documentation

What we focus on

We turn “it seems accurate” into a measurement. We build an evaluation set drawn from your real use cases with known-correct answers, then score outputs systematically for correctness and for hallucination, confident claims with no basis in the source data. For retrieval-based systems we check faithfulness to the provided context specifically. The result is a baseline accuracy number you can track release over release, so you know whether a prompt tweak or model upgrade actually helped or quietly made things worse.
We probe the system the way a motivated user or a bad day would. Structured red-teaming feeds adversarial and edge-case prompts to surface unsafe, off-policy, or manipulable behavior, and fairness testing checks for biased or inconsistent treatment across the groups and scenarios that matter in your context. The point is to find the harmful or embarrassing response in a controlled review, with a fix or a guardrail to follow, rather than discovering it when it’s already in front of a customer or in a screenshot.
A usage policy keeps people from improvising the rules one prompt at a time. We draft a plain-language policy covering approved uses, which data must never be entered, when human review is mandatory, and how to handle AI output in customer-facing or high-stakes work. It’s written to be read and followed, not filed and forgotten, and we tailor it to your tools and risk tolerance so it’s practical, clear enough that a new hire knows the boundaries on day one and specific enough to actually govern behavior.
Models and usage drift, so governance has to continue after launch. We instrument the system to log inputs, outputs, cost, and failures, and build dashboards on the signals that reveal quality slipping: rising error or refusal rates, latency and spend creeping up, and shifts in how people are actually using it. Alerts fire when behavior changes after a provider updates a model or a new use pattern emerges, so a regression is caught and investigated early instead of compounding silently into a trust problem.
When a regulator, board, or customer asks how your AI works and why it can be trusted, you should have a documented answer ready. We produce model cards describing capabilities, limitations, and intended use, decision logs capturing why the system was built the way it was, and an audit trail of behavior and changes over time. This responsible-use documentation demonstrates due diligence, makes the system legible to people who weren’t in the room when it was built, and turns governance from a vague intention into evidence you can show.
