What we focus on

We evaluate every applicable success criterion against WCAG 2.2 Level AA, including the nine new 2.2 criteria such as Focus Not Obscured, Target Size (Minimum), Dragging Movements, and Accessible Authentication. Testing combines automated tooling (axe-core, WAVE, Lighthouse) for coverage with extensive manual inspection, since roughly half of WCAG criteria cannot be verified by automation alone. Each non-conformance is logged with the failing criterion, conformance level, severity, and a recommended fix referencing the relevant W3C technique.
Content is exercised hands-on with multiple screen readers — NVDA and JAWS on Windows, VoiceOver on macOS and iOS — to confirm that name, role, and value are announced correctly and that reading order, headings, landmarks, and live-region updates are coherent aloud. We verify that dynamic components and ARIA usage follow the WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices rather than just passing a parser. Findings note the specific assistive technology and browser pairing so developers can reproduce each issue.
We perform complete keyboard-only walkthroughs to confirm that all functionality is operable without a mouse, that focus order is logical and matches visual order, that focus is always visible, and that no keyboard traps exist. Custom widgets — menus, modals, carousels, comboboxes — are checked against expected ARIA keyboard interaction patterns for arrow, Escape, and Enter handling. Each defect maps to criteria such as 2.1.1 Keyboard, 2.4.3 Focus Order, and 2.4.7 Focus Visible with a concrete remediation.
Text, meaningful graphics, and UI components are measured against WCAG contrast minimums — 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for non-text contrast under 1.4.11 — using tools like the TPGi Colour Contrast Analyser and computed-value sampling. We test against actual rendered states including hover, focus, disabled, and overlay-on-image cases that static palettes overlook. Deliverables include a contrast findings table and adjusted color values that preserve your brand while meeting the threshold.
We produce a completed VPAT 2.5 (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) using the WCAG edition, with honest Supports / Partially Supports / Does Not Support conformance statements and explanatory remarks for each criterion. Alongside it we draft a public-facing accessibility statement modeled on the W3C template, documenting conformance scope, known limitations, and a feedback channel. Both artifacts are written to withstand procurement diligence and legal scrutiny, including ADA Title III and Section 508 contexts.
