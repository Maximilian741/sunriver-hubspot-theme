What we focus on

We build entity-relationship models normalized to third normal form so each fact lives in exactly one place and update anomalies can’t creep in. Where a read path genuinely needs it, we denormalize deliberately, a summary column, a materialized rollup, and write down the tradeoff and how the copy stays consistent, so denormalization is an engineering decision with an owner rather than an accident someone discovers in production a year later.
We give you a clear, defended engine recommendation grounded in your actual access patterns, consistency requirements, and growth curve, not on what’s trending. Relational (Postgres, MySQL) wins when you need joins, multi-row transactions, and strong constraints; a document store like MongoDB fits flexible nested shapes and high write throughput; key-value like Redis fits caching and ephemeral state. Often the right answer is a polyglot split, and we map exactly which data belongs where and why.
We design a partitioning and sharding plan keyed to how the data actually grows so the model still performs at ten times today’s volume. That means native range or list partitioning on time-series and high-volume tables (Postgres declarative partitioning), a partition-pruning and retention strategy so old data ages out cleanly, and where horizontal scale is required, a shard key chosen to spread load evenly and avoid hotspots rather than bolting sharding on after the table is already too big.
We make the database enforce correctness instead of trusting every caller: primary keys, foreign keys with deliberate ON DELETE behavior, UNIQUE and CHECK constraints, NOT NULL, and sensible defaults so invalid states are rejected at the source. We choose key strategy on purpose, surrogate versus natural, and BIGINT or UUID for distributed inserts, so referential integrity holds across the whole application even when a new service or a one-off script writes directly to the tables.
We hand over a model the next engineer can actually understand and extend: an ER diagram, a data dictionary documenting every table, column, type, and meaning, and a written record of the key decisions and the tradeoffs behind them. That documentation lives with the schema and travels with the migrations, so when we leave, your team inherits the reasoning, not a black box they’re afraid to touch.
