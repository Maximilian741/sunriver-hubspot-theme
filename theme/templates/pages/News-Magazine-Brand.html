June 2026 · Compliance · The Lead Story

Section 508, in practice: what an audit actually looks like from the inside

Most organizations meet Section 508 the hard way: a procurement checklist, a deadline three weeks out, and a 90-page PDF that defeats every screen reader on earth. We spent this spring living inside 508 and WCAG 2.2 audit work, and the headline finding is almost boring. The failures are ordinary. They are missing form labels, a focus trap in the “helpful” newsletter modal, contrast that lands at 4.3 when it needed 4.5, and documents exported without a single tag.

Our cycle runs in three passes, and the order is the whole trick. First, an automated scan to clear the rubble; on a good day the tooling catches maybe a third of what is actually wrong. Second, the manual pass that earns the invoice: full keyboard navigation with the mouse unplugged, NVDA and VoiceOver runs, zoom-to-400% and reflow checks, and the document remediation everyone postpones (tagged PDFs, sane reading order, alt text that describes the chart instead of naming the file). Third, the deliverable that actually changes anything: a remediation report ranked by severity and written for the developer who has to fix it on a Tuesday, with the ACR and VPAT documentation procurement teams keep asking for stapled to the back.

The part we are proud of: the latest cycle closed with every critical finding fixed and re-tested, each one confirmed gone rather than parked behind a sad yellow “known issue” flag. If your organization answers to 508, or simply wants WCAG 2.2 AA because locking people out of public services is a bad look, this is the exact work we do.

An accessible product is the version that actually works. The few users you build it for turn out to be everyone.

{# ===== FIELD NOTES (SHORT TAKES) ===== #}
June 2026 · Field Notes

Short takes from the week

  • The shoebox is undefeated. Every Vital Tails test starts the same way: someone digging for a rabies certificate the exact moment they have no signal. Offline-first is the entire point of the app, baked in from the first screen.
  • Automated scanners are a starting gun. The contrast checker passed a button this week that defeats any human eye in daylight. The tools clear the rubble; the manual pass finds the wall.
  • Parity is a feeling before it is a number. Finance teams trust a migrated report the day it matches the old one for a full close cycle. So we test against a full cycle.
  • The best AI answer is sometimes no. A prospect asked us to bolt a chatbot onto a workflow that really needed three fewer steps. We shipped the three fewer steps. That counts as an AI project around here.
